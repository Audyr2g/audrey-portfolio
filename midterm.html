Artifact 1

One Sentence of Grounding: A Three-Configuration Hallucination Analysis of a Retrieval-Augmented Chatbot over Republic Act No. 9003

Title
One Sentence of Grounding: A Three-Configuration Hallucination Analysis of a Retrieval-Augmented Chatbot over Republic Act No. 9003
Author
Audrey Benjamin Gutierrez
Date
9 September 2026
Format
IEEE-format technical report, PDF, 2 pages

The report documents how I built the chatbot and what broke it. Seven chapter files of RA 9003, about 108,000 characters, were split into 239 chunks of 500 characters with 50 of overlap, embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, indexed in ChromaDB, and answered by a Groq-hosted model. The model named in the template had been decommissioned by Groq, so the results were produced on openai/gpt-oss-20b, its recommended replacement.

The lab asked for one stress test that raised the temperature and deleted the fallback sentence at the same time. Because that changes two things at once, I added a third configuration that also deleted the clause restricting the model to the retrieved context. Without the fallback sentence the model still refused, in its own words. Without the restriction clause it answered a question about Venus from its training data in all three trials, with three chunks of waste-management law sitting unused in its context. The report also traces a false refusal on the littering penalty to a chunk boundary that cut the fine away from the offence it belongs to.

Exercise WW-M2

LendSuri at a Glance

A group project proposal for a RAG chatbot that retrieves SEC records on Online Lending Platforms and explains them with sources and dates, rather than issuing a safety verdict.

Research Question

How effectively can a standard RAG-based chatbot retrieve and provide accurate, grounded explanations of available SEC regulatory records and applicable SEC guidance for Online Lending Platforms in the Philippines?

SDG 16 · Target 16.10

Public access to information. The project keeps the SEC, not the language model, as the authority behind every answer.

Knowledge Base

A dated 2026 SEC registry snapshot, the 2021 historical OLP list, MC No. 20 and the 2026 OLP Disclosure Guide, SEC advisories and actions, and SEC complaint procedures.

Language Model

Llama-SEA-LION-v3-8B-IT, an instruction-tuned model for Southeast Asian languages, used without fine-tuning so that new records update the corpus, not the weights.

Evaluation Plan

About 30 test questions kept separate from the knowledge base, scored on retrieval accuracy, answer correctness, groundedness, and handling of unanswerable questions.

Out of Scope

LendSuri does not recommend lenders, guarantee that a platform is safe, investigate crimes, or give loan advice. It reports what SEC records say.

Date

Format
IEEE-format project proposal, PDF, 5 pages

The proposal defines the problem, the users, and the boundaries of LendSuri. It justifies the project under SDG 16, Target 16.10, narrows the cybersecurity domain to one information need, states a single research question, lists the SEC sources for the knowledge base, and sets out how the chatbot will be evaluated.

Its central design decision is a distinction the chatbot must respect: a registered lending company is not the same as a recorded Online Lending Platform, and an older registry entry is not proof of current authorization when a newer SEC advisory exists. LendSuri therefore presents dated evidence with its source, and treats missing evidence as insufficient information rather than as a verdict.

Reflection

Learning Reflection

What these artifacts show about my growth, what I would do differently, and where I am taking this next.

How These Artifacts Demonstrate My Growth

In my Prelim critique I admitted that my experiments changed more than one variable at a time, so I could not say which change caused a result. PT-M1 gave me the same trap in the lab instructions: the stress test raised the temperature and deleted the fallback sentence together. This time I noticed before drawing a conclusion and added a third configuration that changed only the restriction clause. That one extra run is what showed the prompt, not the temperature, decided whether the chatbot stayed grounded.

The Prelim also ended with a lesson I learned the hard way: a model can look excellent on validation data and still generalize poorly. In the LendSuri proposal my group planned for that from the start, keeping the evaluation questions separate from the knowledge base and fixing the expected answer and source passage for each one in advance. The Midterm moved me from tuning a model I was given to defining what a system should and should not do.

Competencies I Developed

I can now build a RAG pipeline end to end with LangChain: load documents, split them with RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter, embed them with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, index them in ChromaDB, and assemble a retrieval chain around a hosted model. I also learned to keep it running when its dependencies move. The template’s model was decommissioned by Groq and its imports broke under LangChain 1.0, and both were fixed in configuration and imports rather than in my own logic.

I learned to scope a research question so it can be measured. LendSuri’s is limited to one platform type, one regulator, and one information need, and it names four measures: retrieval accuracy, answer correctness, groundedness, and the handling of questions the knowledge base cannot answer. Working through the SEC sources also showed me a distinction the chatbot has to respect: a lending company’s SEC registration and an online lending platform’s recording are separate records.

Accomplishments

In PT-M1 I showed that a single clause of plain English was the whole difference between a grounded assistant and an ungrounded one. With the restriction clause removed, the model answered the Venus question from memory in all three trials, and its answers drifted in substance from 735 K to 460–470 °C to 464 °C. Those answers were roughly correct, which is what makes the failure dangerous: nothing tells the user the system has stopped using its documents.

My group completed a five-page IEEE-format proposal and an eight-slide pitch for LendSuri. The decision I would defend most readily is how it treats missing evidence: if a platform is absent from the knowledge base, LendSuri says the evidence is insufficient instead of calling the platform safe or unsafe. The pitch sums this up in its last line: LendSuri doesn’t decide who’s safe; it shows what the SEC says.

Critique of My Own Work and What I Would Do Differently

My chunking ignored the structure of the law. The littering fine was split from its offence because Section 49 refers back to Section 48 only by cross-reference, and fixed 500-character chunks cut straight through it. Raising k did not help. I should have split the statute by Section from the start, since the corpus was already organised by chapter.

My stress test was narrow. Each configuration ran three times on one out-of-domain question. That was enough to show the restriction clause matters, but not to say how often a grounded configuration still slips. A set of out-of-domain and near-domain questions would have given a rate instead of an example.

The LendSuri proposal has not been tested yet. All four of its measures are plans, and its planned test set of about thirty questions has to cover several case types and languages. That leaves only a few questions per case, the same small-sample weakness I found in the Prelim. I would report results per case type with the count beside each score.

Short-Term Goals

Before the Finals, I want to apply what PT-M1 taught me to LendSuri: keep the restriction clause and the source printout in every version of the prompt, treat the prompt as a versioned file, and test chunk sizes on the LendSuri records instead of assuming a default. I also want to check early whether the model answers Filipino and Taglish questions as reliably as English ones, since the proposal assumes it can.

Long-Term Goals

PT-M1 made the risk concrete: a chatbot can give a confident, correct-sounding answer with sources printed beside it that have nothing to do with the answer. A borrower using LendSuri may act on what it says with real money. Over the rest of my degree I want to build systems that show their evidence and say plainly when they do not know — and to measure that behaviour instead of assuming it.
