This page presents my Midterm work for ITC-C508. It centres on LendSuri, a cybersecurity-focused Retrieval-Augmented Generation chatbot that my group proposed for explaining SEC regulatory records on Philippine Online Lending Platforms.

In Exercise WW-M2 my group — Stephen Rainiel Adame, Mica Ellah Mae Nerido, and I — proposed LendSuri. Its name comes from the Filipino word suri, “to examine closely”. The project responds to a specific information problem: a Filipino borrower who finds an online lending app cannot easily tell what official SEC records say about it, because that evidence is spread across a current registry, older lists, and later advisories.

The proposal and the pitch deck present the same plan in two forms: a full IEEE-format paper and an eight-slide presentation.

LendSuri at a Glance

A group project proposal for a RAG chatbot that retrieves SEC records on Online Lending Platforms and explains them with sources and dates, rather than issuing a safety verdict.

Research Question

How effectively can a standard RAG-based chatbot retrieve and provide accurate, grounded explanations of available SEC regulatory records and applicable SEC guidance for Online Lending Platforms in the Philippines?

SDG 16 · Target 16.10

Public access to information. The project keeps the SEC, not the language model, as the authority behind every answer.

Knowledge Base

A dated 2026 SEC registry snapshot, the 2021 historical OLP list, MC No. 20 and the 2026 OLP Disclosure Guide, SEC advisories and actions, and SEC complaint procedures.

Language Model

Llama-SEA-LION-v3-8B-IT, an instruction-tuned model for Southeast Asian languages, used without fine-tuning so that new records update the corpus, not the weights.

Evaluation Plan

About 30 test questions kept separate from the knowledge base, scored on retrieval accuracy, answer correctness, groundedness, and handling of unanswerable questions.

Out of Scope

LendSuri does not recommend lenders, guarantee that a platform is safe, investigate crimes, or give loan advice. It reports what SEC records say.

Date
5 October 2026
Format
IEEE-format project proposal, PDF, 5 pages

The proposal defines the problem, the users, and the boundaries of LendSuri. It justifies the project under SDG 16, Target 16.10, narrows the cybersecurity domain to one information need, states a single research question, lists the SEC sources for the knowledge base, and sets out how the chatbot will be evaluated.

Its central design decision is a distinction the chatbot must respect: a registered lending company is not the same as a recorded Online Lending Platform, and an older registry entry is not proof of current authorization when a newer SEC advisory exists. LendSuri therefore presents dated evidence with its source, and treats missing evidence as insufficient information rather than as a verdict.

LendSuri Proposal Pitch Deck

Title
LendSuri — Project Proposal Pitch
Authors
Stephen Rainiel Adame, Mica Ellah Mae Nerido, and Audrey Benjamin Gutierrez
Date
5 October 2026
Format
PowerPoint presentation, 8 slides

The pitch deck condenses the proposal into eight slides: the problem, the target audience, the research question, the knowledge base, the language model, what is out of scope, and the evaluation plan.

It grounds the problem in three figures: 23% of TransUnion’s 2025 general-population sample used money-lending apps, PAOCC received 47,446 complaints about allegedly abusive online lending apps between August 2024 and January 2026, and three agencies — DICT, NPC, and SEC — issued a joint advisory in March 2026. It closes on the project’s position in one line: LendSuri does not decide who is safe; it shows what the SEC says.
