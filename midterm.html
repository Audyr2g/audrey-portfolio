Exercise WW-M2

LendSuri at a Glance

A group project proposal for a RAG chatbot that retrieves SEC records on Online Lending Platforms and explains them with sources and dates, rather than issuing a safety verdict.

Research Question

How effectively can a standard RAG-based chatbot retrieve and provide accurate, grounded explanations of available SEC regulatory records and applicable SEC guidance for Online Lending Platforms in the Philippines?

SDG 16 · Target 16.10

Public access to information. The project keeps the SEC, not the language model, as the authority behind every answer.

Knowledge Base

A dated 2026 SEC registry snapshot, the 2021 historical OLP list, MC No. 20 and the 2026 OLP Disclosure Guide, SEC advisories and actions, and SEC complaint procedures.

Language Model

Llama-SEA-LION-v3-8B-IT, an instruction-tuned model for Southeast Asian languages, used without fine-tuning so that new records update the corpus, not the weights.

Evaluation Plan

About 30 test questions kept separate from the knowledge base, scored on retrieval accuracy, answer correctness, groundedness, and handling of unanswerable questions.

Out of Scope

LendSuri does not recommend lenders, guarantee that a platform is safe, investigate crimes, or give loan advice. It reports what SEC records say.

Format
IEEE-format project proposal, PDF, 5 pages

The proposal defines the problem, the users, and the boundaries of LendSuri. It justifies the project under SDG 16, Target 16.10, narrows the cybersecurity domain to one information need, states a single research question, lists the SEC sources for the knowledge base, and sets out how the chatbot will be evaluated.

Its central design decision is a distinction the chatbot must respect: a registered lending company is not the same as a recorded Online Lending Platform, and an older registry entry is not proof of current authorization when a newer SEC advisory exists. LendSuri therefore presents dated evidence with its source, and treats missing evidence as insufficient information rather than as a verdict.

Reflection

Learning Reflection

What these artifacts show about my growth, what I would do differently, and where I am taking this next.

How These Artifacts Demonstrate My Growth

The Prelim ended with a lesson I learned the hard way: a model can look excellent on validation data and still generalize poorly. The Midterm was the first chance I had to act on that before running anything. In the LendSuri proposal, the evaluation questions are kept separate from the knowledge base, and the expected answer and source passage for each question are set in advance. In my Prelim critique I wrote that I should have fixed my selection rule before my experiments; in this project the rule is part of the plan from the start.

The kind of problem also changed. In the Prelim I was given a task and tried to score well on it. In WW-M2 my group had to define the task ourselves: pick a domain, narrow cybersecurity down to one information need, and decide what the chatbot would refuse to do. Much of the proposal is about those boundaries rather than about the model.

Competencies I Developed

I can now explain how a Retrieval-Augmented Generation system divides the work. The retriever finds the relevant documents, and the language model explains what is in them. LendSuri depends on that split. SEC records change over time, so the plan updates the knowledge base when new records appear and leaves the model, Llama-SEA-LION-v3-8B-IT, without any fine-tuning.

I also learned to scope a research question so it can be measured. Ours is limited to one platform type, one regulator, and one information need, and it names four measures: retrieval accuracy, answer correctness, groundedness, and the handling of questions the knowledge base cannot answer. Working through SEC Memorandum Circular No. 20 and the 2026 OLP Disclosure Guide showed me a distinction the chatbot has to respect. A lending company's SEC registration and an online lending platform's recording are separate records, so one cannot stand in for the other.

Accomplishments

My group completed a five-page IEEE-format proposal and an eight-slide pitch for LendSuri, a chatbot that retrieves SEC records on Philippine online lending platforms and explains them with their sources and dates. The project is framed under SDG 16, Target 16.10, which concerns public access to information, and the problem is grounded in figures such as the 47,446 complaints about allegedly abusive lending apps that PAOCC received between August 2024 and January 2026.

The decision I would defend most readily is how LendSuri treats missing or outdated evidence. If a platform is absent from the knowledge base, the chatbot says the evidence is insufficient instead of calling the platform safe or unsafe. An older registry entry is shown alongside any newer SEC advisory, so it is never presented as proof of current authorization on its own. The pitch sums this up in its last line: LendSuri doesn’t decide who’s safe; it shows what the SEC says.

Critique of My Own Work and What I Would Do Differently

Nothing in the proposal has been tested yet. All four measures are plans, and the deck itself calls the model a preliminary choice whose performance will be measured, not assumed. Until the evaluation runs, the proposal is a design argument. I would have liked at least a small retrieval test on a handful of SEC records before submitting, so that one claim in the paper rested on a result.

The planned test set is small. About thirty questions have to cover current-registry cases, historical records, later SEC actions, unanswerable questions, and Filipino or Taglish phrasing. That leaves only a few questions per case type, which is the same weakness I found in my Prelim, where twelve test examples could swing the F1-score by 0.15 to 0.20. I would either grow the set or report results per case type with the count beside each score, so a reader can see how much weight each number can carry.

The knowledge base is a snapshot. The registry data is dated on purpose so the experiment can be reproduced, but a borrower reading an answer may assume it is current. The proposal keeps a documented cutoff date; I would go further and have every answer state that date, so the limitation reaches the user and not only the paper.

The hardware question is still open. The deck mentions a quantized model or hosted inference as ways around limited Colab GPU memory, but we did not choose between them. Quantization can change answer quality, so I would settle this early and run the evaluation on the exact setup we intend to use.

Short-Term Goals

Before the Finals period, I want to build the knowledge base with its snapshot date recorded for every source, and to write the evaluation questions, expected answers, and source passages before the first retrieval run, so the test set cannot drift toward what the system happens to do well. I also want to check early whether the model answers Filipino and Taglish questions as reliably as English ones, since the proposal assumes it can.

Long-Term Goals

The Prelim made me skeptical of a single good score. LendSuri moved that skepticism toward the person reading the output: a borrower may act on a chatbot’s answer with real money. Over the rest of my degree I want to build systems that show their evidence and say plainly when they do not know — and to measure that behaviour instead of assuming it.
