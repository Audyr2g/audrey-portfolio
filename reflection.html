ITC-C508 · Prelim to Finals

Course Reflection

Each period of ITC-C508 left me with a working system and a list of ways it could fail. This page puts those failures in the places where they would matter: a support desk, a barangay office, and a borrower holding a loan app.

Prelim

My Prelim classifier sorted airline messages into praise, complaints and urgent support requests. If a support desk relied on it, the sarcastic complaint it read as Positive_Praise would have landed in the wrong queue. My Keras models had a quieter blind spot: five of them tied on validation, but their test scores ranged from 0.5281 to 0.8296, and with only twelve test examples nothing told me which one to trust.

If I built it for real I would do a few things differently. I’d train on far more than the 75 examples I had, put sarcastic and ambiguous messages into the test set on purpose, and check how the model handles urgent requests on their own before trusting one overall accuracy number. My PT-P1 model also pushed general inquiries into one of its three labels because it had nowhere else to put them. A real support tool would need a way to say a message doesn’t fit any category and pass it to a person.

Midterm

My RA 9003 chatbot refused to give the fine for littering because a chunk had split the amount from the offence, so a barangay officer asking it would get no answer even though the law has one. The worse case came when I removed one line from the prompt: it answered a question about Venus from memory while showing chunks of the waste law as its sources. Anyone reading that would trust an answer the documents never gave.

LendSuri, our group proposal, carried the same worry over to money. A borrower who finds a lending app on Facebook wants to know whether the SEC has it on record, and a confident wrong answer could cost them. That’s why we planned for LendSuri to show the dated SEC records with their sources and to say when the evidence isn’t enough, instead of calling any app safe. If I made the RA 9003 chatbot again, I’d split the law by Section so a fine always stays with its offence, and I’d treat the prompt as something to test, since deleting one line was enough to break it.

Finals

With LendSuri, a borrower asking about Peso2you would never get the right record, because the retriever kept returning other apps with Peso, Cash or Loan in their names. When lenders’ names get copied, that kind of mix-up is the last thing a borrower needs. Speed mattered too: the free Colab CPU didn’t finish one answer in about 40 minutes, and nobody checking a loan app would wait that long.

In my PT-F1 test the model at least refused to answer when the right record wasn’t retrieved, which is better than naming the wrong company. A real version of LendSuri would still need a retriever that also matches exact app names, and many more than nine test questions before anyone relied on it. Our group only got usable speeds on the L4 GPU, so running something like LendSuri for real means planning for that hardware from the start. We also didn’t save the generated answers, and that is the first thing I’d fix, because without them nobody can check why a result came out the way it did.
